# Cancun airport to Dreams Jade

Priced 3 November 2026, for six people landing together on AM 536 at **18:36
on Sunday 3 January** and leaving together on **Friday 8 January**.

Dreams Jade is in Puerto Morelos, about 20 to 27 km south of the airport
depending on who is measuring, and roughly 20 to 27 minutes by road.

## The answer

**Book one private van, round trip, through an open-market operator. About
$100 for the vehicle, $16.67 each.**

The whole group is on one flight, which is the thing that makes this cheap.
A van is priced per vehicle, not per head, so six people splitting one van is
the lowest sensible cost per person available on this route. Split the group
across two arrivals and it becomes two vans and roughly double.

Quoted on eTransfers' own booking engine on 3 November for 3 to 8 January at
six passengers: a Toyota Hiace class van, up to 8 passengers and 8 bags,
private and never shared, flight monitoring, driver meets you past customs
with a sign. Taxes, tolls and luggage are included and the price does not
change if the flight lands late.

**Take the $100 Premium fare, not the $94 Economy one.** The $6 across the
group buys free cancellation up to 24 hours before pickup and free changes up
to 12 hours before. Nothing about the beach leg is committed yet, so a
transfer that can be unwound for nothing is worth $1 a head.

**Holiday week does not move this price.** The same $94 shows for a random
November date, which is unusual enough on this trip to be worth writing down.

## Uber is not an option here

Do not plan on it, and do not let anybody arrive expecting to open the app.

Mexico's transport ministry has said Uber is not permitted to operate from
Cancun airport, and the airport has posted notices to that effect. Uber won an
injunction that stops drivers being fined, but that did not create a pickup
area or oblige the airport to provide one, so the legal position and the
practical position are different things. The airport operator's contracts give
the taxi unions the exclusive right to pick up at arrivals.

In practice this means drivers decline airport pickups, or ask you to walk
out to a road, and there are repeated reports of unlicensed operators
approaching arriving tourists and telling them platforms are banned.

**Uber Taxi**, Uber's airport product, does work: it dispatches a licensed
union taxi through the app. But it is priced at taxi rates, not UberX rates,
so it buys the app's convenience and none of its savings. DiDi has the same
problem.

Uber is genuinely useful once you are in Puerto Morelos or Playa del Carmen.
It is the airport specifically that is the problem, in both directions.

## Why not the bus

ADO runs an airport service and some departures stop at Puerto Morelos, at
roughly 210 pesos, about $12 a head. For two people with a backpack each it
would be the obvious call.

It is wrong for this group. Six people with five nights of luggage, landing at
18:36 in the dark, dropped at the highway junction rather than at the resort,
and then needing taxis from the junction to the hotel anyway. The saving
against a van is a few dollars each and it costs an hour and a half of
everybody's first evening. Sources also disagree about whether the airport
service still stops at Puerto Morelos at all, which is not a thing to discover
with six people and the bags.

## AMSTAR, and why it is not the default

AMSTAR is the resort's authorised operator and the only one Dreams Jade named.
Contact details are in `private/jade-quote.md`.

It publishes no rate anywhere, which is itself informative. The reports that
do exist are not encouraging: one traveller describes paying $180 round trip
for a family of four against a $72 taxi quote for the same journey, and its
public reviews run poorly.

**It was emailed on 3 November and answered on 4 November with $184 round
trip**, so the question is now settled with a written number rather than a
report. Sandra Flores, Inside Sales, quoting a van rated 1 to 6 passengers:

| | |
|---|---|
| Price | $184 round trip for the vehicle, $30.67 a head at six |
| Vehicle | Van, capacity 1 to 6 |
| Luggage | Rate assumes one piece per person |
| Taxes | Included |
| Gratuity | Not included |
| Cancellation | 48 hours before the service date |
| Payment | In full at booking |
| Flight monitoring | Yes, and notify them if the flight number changes |

So AMSTAR is $84 above eTransfers on the vehicle for the same journey, on
worse cancellation terms, with a luggage allowance of one bag a head against
eight bags in the van. The resort's own operator loses on every line that was
compared. Three of the five questions asked were answered; the meet-and-greet
point at Terminal 4 and whether anything is payable to the driver were not,
and neither was the direct question about the price gap.

The quote is also now out of date, because it was written against a return
that no longer exists. See below.

## The return splits, and that changes both quotes

Both quotes were asked for against one return pickup at 15:15. That is wrong.
On Friday 8 January the group leaves Cancun on two flights:

| Flight | Departs CUN | Passengers |
|---|---|---|
| DL 641 | 13:50 | 1, Christian, routing home through Atlanta |
| DL 1911 | 15:15 | 4, Krysta, Ryan, Haley and Eddie |
| not yet booked | unknown | 1, Bubba |

That looks like two pickups on the departure morning, which would mean a
second fare, because a van is priced per vehicle per run.

**The intended answer is one van anyway.** The two departures are 85 minutes
apart, which is inside the slack that a Cancun international departure needs
regardless. So the plan is a single pickup sized to the earlier flight, with
everybody waiting at the airport together.

| | |
|---|---|
| Earlier flight | DL 641 at 13:50 |
| Three hours before it | 10:50 at the airport |
| Resort to CUN | 27 km, about 27 minutes |
| **Van leaves Dreams Jade** | **about 10:15, provisional** |
| Checkout | 12:00, so everybody checks out early rather than at noon |

What it costs: the four on the 15:15 get about four and a half hours at the
airport instead of the three the itinerary assumed. What it saves: a second
vehicle, a second fare, and coordinating two pickups on a checkout morning.
At six people splitting one vehicle that trade is worth taking.

**Do not shave the 13:50 margin to shorten the wait.** Christian's Atlanta
connection on the way home is one hour twenty five minutes, the thinnest
thing in anybody's itinerary, so he is the passenger with the least room to
absorb a slow security queue at Cancun.

This also takes Bubba's unreported return mostly out of the question. Any
Friday departure at or after 15:15 fits the same single run. Only something
earlier than 13:50 would break it and put a second vehicle back on the table.

The arrival is unaffected either way: all six on AM 536 landing 18:36, one
van.

A follow-up to AMSTAR is drafted and waiting to send. It states the split,
says the group would prefer one pickup timed to the 13:50, and asks whether
that is still $184 or whether an earlier pickup carries a surcharge. It also
asks what two separate pickups would cost as a fallback, asks for a
recommended pickup time against the 13:50, asks whether one vehicle can make
two terminal drop-offs on one run, and puts the three unanswered questions
again. Do not book either operator until that answer is in: the whole point
of the comparison is to compare the same journey.

## What has to be true for the $100 to hold

- All six on AM 536, landing 18:36. One arrival, one van.
- Six passengers and six people's luggage inside a Hiace rated for 8 and 8.
  Fine, but not if anybody brings a board or a large case beyond the usual.
- Booked ahead rather than arranged on the kerb. The kerb price is higher and
  the kerb is where the pressure selling happens.
