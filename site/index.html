Two things worth knowing

The beach build is settled: four Ocean View Kings. Settled on 2 November 2026 in favour of four rooms. Kept because it is the reasoning behind the number everybody is being asked to pay. Six adults as two couples and two singles can be housed in three rooms or in four. Three rooms puts the two singles together in a Junior Suite Two Double, which is two separate beds and breaks no rule, at $966 each. Four rooms gives each single their own Junior Suite at $1,500 each. The fourth room is what costs money here. It is $534 more for each single, $1,067 to the group, which is more than four times what upgrading a couple from a standard suite to Ocean Front costs. The ocean view is the cheap part of this decision and the fourth room is the expensive part, which is the opposite of how it looks. Four won. Both single travellers get a door that closes, and that premium was paid deliberately rather than by accident.

Mexico City is committed. Free for 24 hours after booking, then partial refund only until 16:00 on 22 December 2026, listing local time. The free window has already closed. Until 22 December only a partial refund is available and after that nothing, so the money is committed. The second payment of $2,251.42 lands on 14 December, eight days ahead of that cliff.

Estimates are never added to a total

Every total on this page counts only the lines marked booked or quoted. Lines marked estimate are never added in. The totals are what you owe Ryan, because he fronted the bookings on one card and everybody settles up with him. There are two kinds of estimate here and they are not the same thing. The estimates blocks are money you spend yourself on the ground, in cash or on your own card, and the figure is there so the week does not surprise anybody. The owed_estimates block is money that lands on Ryan's card and that you will therefore owe him, but which cannot be pinned to a number yet. Only one line is in it: drinks and tip at Pujol, because the restaurant has no pairing and the bill depends on what the table orders. Keeping it out of the total is the rule working correctly, not an oversight. Treating the total as the final Pujol number is the mistake to avoid.
